The subsequent table provides further TRIZ principles with their definitions and examples/applications:
*   **Segmentation (1):** Divide an object into independent parts; Make an object easy to disassemble; Increase the degree of fragmentation or segmentation; Transition to micro-level. Example: Concept of modular furniture where the components of the furniture are disassembled at the point of purchase and during transport. The furniture is then assembled at point of use. The advantages of Segmentation are that it saves space and is easy to transport. Segmentation is extensively used in DIY (Do-It-Yourself) concept. Segmentation can also be applied for cost-saving benefits e.g. replacement of worn-out part of tool or equipment rather than entire whole.
*   **Taking Out (Extraction) (2):** Separate an interfering part or property from an object, or single out the only necessary part (or property) of an object. Examples: Small glass ampoules of medicine are sealed by flame, but the heat from the flame can degrade the medicine. To avoid the heat from the flame, immerse the ampoules in water to keep the medicine at a safe temperature. The compressor of the air conditioning unit which is noisy and emits heat, is "taken out" to the outside of building.
*   **Local Quality (3):** Change an object's structure from uniform to non-uniform, change an external environment (or external influence) from uniform to non-uniform; Make each part of an object function in conditions most suitable for its operation; Make each part of an object fulfill a different and useful function. Examples: Vacuum cleaner which has dedicated parts for moving dust, filtering out dust, and storing dust. Lunch box with special compartments for hot/cold and solid/liquid foods. Pencil with eraser, claw hammer.
*   **Asymmetry (4):** Change the shape of an object from symmetrical to asymmetrical; If an object is asymmetrical, increase its degree of asymmetry. Example: If you were to observe the surroundings, you will find that it is very difficult to find any object which has a non-symmetrical outline. Why is that so? Psychologically, when we see a symmetrical outline, our brain is programmed to assume that this is perfect. Similarly, almost all objects have a symmetrical outline and some we will judge them as imperfect or defective. This principle reminds us not to be influenced with symmetry being the solution but to look at asymmetry as an alternative which may potentially solve the problem.
*   **Merging (5):** Bring closer together (or merge) identical or similar objects; assemble identical or similar parts to perform parallel operations; Make operations contiguous or parallel; bring them together in time. Examples: Millions of transistors fabricated together to form a microprocessor. Many rowers in a boat work together to move the boat faster and further.
*   **Universality (Multi-functionality) (6):** Make a part or object perform multiple functions; eliminate the need for other parts; Use standardized features. Examples: Camper van which consists of bed, kitchen, sofa. Child's car safety seat converts to a stroller or a large carry-on basket. Smartphone with features of a PDA (Personal Digital Assistant), GPS (Global Positioning System), Wi-Fi and digital camera. Toilet with a heated seat, auto water jet and flowing water for cleaning hands.
*   **Nested Doll (7):** Place one object inside another; place each object, in turn, inside the other; Make one part pass through a cavity in the other. Examples: Antenna of a car which can be retracted or extended. Tripod stand for camera which can be retracted or extended. Containers with identical shapes but different sizes which can be placed into each other.
*   **Anti-weight (8):** To compensate for the weight of an object; merge it with other objects that provide lift; To compensate for the weight of an object, make it interact with the environment (e.g. use aerodynamic, hydrodynamic, buoyancy and other forces). Examples: Helium balloon for advertising. Capillary action to draw solder from printed circuit board joint to soldering iron tip.
*   **Preliminary Anti-action / Prior counter-action (9):** If it will be necessary to do an action with both harmful and useful effects, this action should be replaced with anti-actions to control harmful effects; Create beforehand stresses in an object that will oppose known undesirable working stresses later on. Examples: The use of sleeping pills can pose a risk of overdose. Thus, certain sleeping pill manufacturers introduce a vomit inducing ingredient into each sleeping pill such that it induces vomiting of the ingested sleeping pills, if the vomit inducing ingredient exceeds a certain threshold. This prevents an overdose.
*   **Preliminary Action (Prior action - "Do it in advance") (10):** Perform the required change of an object (either fully or partially) before it is needed; Pre-arrange objects such that they can come into action from the most convenient place and without losing time for their delivery. Examples: Marinate food before a barbecue party. Pre-pack food into containers prior to a picnic.
*   **Beforehand Cushioning (11):** Prepare emergency means beforehand to compensate for the relatively low reliability of an object. Example: Back-up parachute is used to compensate for the low reliability of the main parachute.
*   **Equipotentiality (Remove tension) (12):** In a potential field, limit position changes (e.g. change operating conditions to eliminate the need to raise or lower objects in a gravity field). Example: Panama Canal is a major shipping canal which connects the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans. Since the water levels of the ocean and lake are different, water elevators are used to raise or lower the ships from ocean to lake and vice versa to achieve equipotentiality, as they cross the Panama Canal.
*   **The Other Way Around (13):** Invert the action(s) used to solve the problem (e.g. instead of cooling an object, heat it); Make movable parts (or the external environment) fixed, and fixed parts movable; Turn the object (or process) 'upside down'. Examples: Change the magnetic crane for moving vehicles in a scrap yard from electromagnet to a permanent magnet to hold the load. Electrical power is needed only when releasing the load by activating a counter electromagnet. A free added safety benefit is that the load is supported even during a power failure. Escalator or moving sidewalk with standing people. In order to avoid using expensive lifting equipment to change the engine oil of the car, a pit which is below the road surface allows workers to change the engine oil with ease.
*   **Curvature (14):** Instead of using rectilinear parts, surfaces, or forms, use curvilinear ones; move from flat surfaces to spherical ones; from parts shaped as a cube (parallelepiped) to ball-shaped structures; Use rollers, balls, spirals, domes; Go from linear to rotary motion, use centrifugal forces. Examples: In order to avoid clogging of pen with thick ink, a tiny metal ball which is rotated at the end of the pen tip is used. The ball would act as a cap to keep the ink from drying and allows ink to flow out of the pen at a controlled rate. Change in semiconductor packaging technology from rectilinear leads and pins to spherical balls.
*   **Dynamization (15):** Allow (or design) the characteristics of an object, external environment, or process to change to be optimal for or to find an optimal operating condition; Divide an object into parts capable of movement relative to each other; If an object (or process) is rigid or inflexible, make it movable or adaptive. Examples: Table with movable sections which can expand to accommodate from 4 chairs to 8 chairs. Adjustable steering wheel for different heights.
*   **Partial or Excessive Action (16):** If 100 percent of an object is hard to achieve using a given solution method then, by using 'slightly less' or 'slightly more' of the same method, the problem may be considerably easier to solve. Example: In screen printing, excessive ink is applied on stencil and a squeegee is used to wipe off the excess ink.
*   **Another Dimension (17):** Move an object in two- or three-dimensional space; Use a multi-storey arrangement of objects instead of a single-storey arrangement; Tilt or re-orient the object, lay it on its side; Use 'another side' of a given area. Examples: 2D bar-code on food packaging. CD changer cassette with many stacked CD's. Multi-layer stacked die or stacked packages in semiconductor packaging.
*   **Mechanical Vibration / Oscillation (18):** Cause an object to oscillate or vibrate; Increase its frequency (even up to the ultrasonic); Use an object's resonant frequency; Use piezoelectric vibrators instead of mechanical ones; Use combined ultrasonic and electromagnetic field oscillations. Examples: Ultrasonic cleaning. Break gallstones into smaller pieces using ultrasonic resonance.
*   **Periodic Action (19):** Instead of continuous action, use periodic or pulsating actions; If an action is already periodic, change the periodic magnitude or frequency; Use pauses between impulses to perform a different action. Examples: Pulsating water jet is used to break concrete. In cardio-pulmonary resuscitation (CPR), blow into victim's mouth after every 5 chest compressions.
*   **Continuity of Useful Action (20):** Carry on work continuously; make all parts of an object work at full load, all the time; Eliminate all idle or intermittent actions or work. Examples: Operate manufacturing facility at 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, to maximize tool usage. Print during the return of a printer carriage in inkjet printers.
*   **Skipping (Rushing Through / Hurrying) (21):** Conduct a process, or certain stages (e.g. destructive, harmful or hazardous operations) at high speed. Example: To avoid heating gum tissues, use a high-speed dentist's drill.
*   **Blessing in Disguise ("Turn lemons into lemonade") (22):** Use harmful factors (particularly, harmful effects of the environment or surroundings) to achieve a positive effect; Eliminate the primary harmful action by adding it to another harmful action to resolve the problem; Amplify a harmful factor to such a degree that it is no longer harmful. Examples: High voltage burn-in stress to screen out infant mortality failures in semiconductor manufacturing. Start a fire deliberately in order to check a forest fire by creating a barren area in advance of it.
*   **Feedback (23):** Introduce feedback (referring back, cross-checking) to improve a process or action; If feedback is already used, change its magnitude or influence. Examples: Statistical Process Control (SPC) to determine whether process is drifting out of control. Thermostat for optimal temperature stabilization.
*   **Intermediary (24):** Use an intermediary carrier article or intermediary process; Merge one object temporarily with another. Examples: Thermal insulating gloves to carry hot objects. In Japan's vending machines, a layer of foam is sprayed onto the cup's outer layer of hot drink to prevent from burning one's hand.
*   **Self-Service (25):** Make an object serve itself by performing auxiliary helpful functions; Use waste resources, energy, or substances. Examples: Vending machines to dispense drinks or food items. Convert waste food to fertilizer.
*   **Copying (26):** Instead of an unavailable, expensive, fragile object, use simpler and inexpensive copies; Replace an object, or process with optical copies; If visible optical copies are already used, move to infrared or ultraviolet copies. Examples: Listen to an audio CD instead of physically attending a conference. Use infrared scanners to detect hot spots in electrical circuitry.x
*   **Cheap Short-living Objects (Cheap Disposables) (27):** Replace an expensive object with a multiple of inexpensive objects, comprising certain qualities (such as service life, for instance). Examples: Disposable kitchen paper towel. Plastic cups/spoons/forks. Disposable diapers and medical supplies (e.g. syringes, wipes).
*   **Mechanics Substitution (28):** Replace a mechanical means with a sensory (optical, acoustic, taste or smell) means; Use electric, magnetic and electromagnetic fields to interact with the object; Change from static to movable fields, from unstructured fields to those having structure; Use fields in conjunction with field-activated (e.g. ferromagnetic) particles. Example: In order to mix two powders together, electrostatically charge each powder with an opposite charge. Bar coding.
*   **Pneumatics and Hydraulics (29):** Use gas and liquid parts of an object instead of solid parts (e.g. inflatable, filled with liquids, air cushion, hydrostatic, hydro-reactive). Examples: Padding inside shoes which are filled with gel. Vacuum pick and place equipment.
*   **Flexible Shells and Thin Films (30):** Use flexible shells and thin films instead of three dimensional structures; Isolate the object from the external environment using flexible shells and thin films. Examples: Laminated safety glass for car windshields consists of a sandwich of a thin layer of flexible clear plastic film between two or more pieces of glass. The plastic film holds the glass in place when the glass breaks, helping to lessen injuries from flying glass shards. Plastic bags, plastic wraps.
*   **Porous Materials (31):** Make an object porous or add porous elements (inserts, coatings, etc.); If an object is already porous, use the pores to introduce a useful substance or function. Example: Since a solid brick is heavy, introduce holes (pores) in the brick to reduce its weight while still maintaining its strength.
*   **Color Changes (32):** Change the color of an object or its external environment; Change the transparency of an object or its external environment; In order to improve observability of things that are difficult to see, use colored additives or luminescent elements. Examples: In order to ensure that Zip Lock bags are properly sealed, have one side in blue color and another in yellow color. When the bags are zipped properly, it is indicated by green color when blue and yellow sides overlap each other. Yellow light in photolithography room for wafer fabrication.
*   **Homogeneity (Uniformity) (33):** Make objects interact with a given object of the same material (or material with identical properties). Examples: Make a diamond cutting tool out of diamond. Use sand blasting to create an imprint on glass since both materials are made of silicon.
*   **Discarding and Recovering (34):** Make portions of an object that have fulfilled their functions go away (discard by dissolving, evaporating, etc.) or modify these directly during operation; Conversely, restore consumable parts of an object directly in operation. Examples: Dissolving capsule for medicine. Deionized water recycling system.
*   **Parameter Changes (35):** Change an object's physical state (e.g. to a gas, liquid, or solid); Change the concentration or consistency; Change the degree of flexibility; Change the temperature; Change the pressure. Examples: Pressurize nitrogen gas into liquid for reduced volume while stored and transported in tanks. Use refrigerator to lower the temperature of food.
*   **Phase Transitions (36):** Use phenomena occurring during phase transitions (e.g. volume changes, loss or absorption of heat). Examples: Refrigeration system uses concept of excess heat removal through heat of vaporization and condensation. Solid, liquid, gas e.g. dry ice from solid state to gaseous state.
*   **Thermal Expansion (37):** Use thermal expansion (or contraction) of materials; If thermal expansion is being used, use multiple materials with different coefficients of thermal expansion. Example: Bi-metallic strip/sensor.
*   **Strong Oxidants (Enriched Atmosphere / Accelerated) (38):** Replace common air with oxygen-enriched air; Replace enriched air with pure oxygen; Expose air or oxygen to ionizing radiation; Use ionized oxygen; Replace ozonized (or ionized) oxygen with ozone. Examples: Use air ionizers to create a mixture of charged positive and negative air particles to reduce electrostatic charging. Use oxy-acetylene torch for cutting at very high temperature.
*   **Inert Atmosphere (39):** Replace a normal environment with an inert one; Add neutral parts, or inert additives to an object. Examples: Nitrogen gas is used to fill up airplane tires to prevent freezing at high altitudes. This is due to the fact that nitrogen is an inert gas, so high temperature and pressure changes have less effect. Detergent or medicine has very little active material and thus is very difficult to weigh or measure. Inert substances are added to detergent or medicine to provide bulk and make it easier to weigh or measure.
*   **Composite Materials (40):** Change from uniform to composite (multiple) materials. Example: Most modern golf club shafts are made of either steel or a carbon-fiber and resin composite. Carbon fiber which is a composite material has the advantage of being lighter than steel.
